# The FM channel

Every port in net-sim is an FM radio, and the air between them is a physical FM channel. This page says what the model does, where its numbers come from, how it was checked against real radios, and where it falls short.

## What happens to a transmission

1. The TNC's transmit audio goes through its radio's transmit path: on the voice path, pre-emphasis, the limiter and a 300-3000 Hz filter; on the data path, straight in.
2. It is frequency-modulated onto a carrier. Full-scale audio gives the radio's rated deviation (2.5, 4 or 5 kHz), so a TNC that transmits quieter deviates less.
3. Each receiving port gets every carrier that reaches it, at its received level (transmit power minus path loss) and frequency offset, plus its own noise: thermal noise in its IF bandwidth, times its noise figure and its site's man-made noise.
4. The receiver filters to its IF bandwidth, then a limiter and discriminator turn the sum back into audio, which goes through the receive path (de-emphasis and the voice filter, or the flat data tap) to the TNC.

Nothing in the router decides who captures a receiver, what a collision sounds like, or how noisy a weak signal is. Those come out of steps 3 and 4.

A transmitting radio's own receiver is muted. With the squelch open a receiver hears hiss whenever nobody is transmitting; a signal-strength squelch mutes it until the received power crosses its threshold.

## Where the model comes from

The DSP is a Go port of [M0LTE.FmChannel](https://github.com/M0LTE/M0LTE.FmChannel) 0.7.0, the FM link pdn-soundmodem measures its modes through. That package puts one burst on one carrier at a stated carrier-to-noise ratio; net-sim runs the same stages continuously, in 10 ms blocks, with any number of carriers per receiver.

`tools/fmref` generates reference vectors from the published package, and `internal/fm`'s tests hold the port to them:

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Noiseless output, data port 12.5 kHz, data port 25 kHz, mic/speaker | Matches to -108 dB or better (float rounding) |
| SINAD of a 1 kHz tone, CNR -2 to 30 dB | Within sampling error of the reference |
| Output noise under an unmodulated carrier, CNR -2 to 30 dB | Within 0.8 dB |

One difference is deliberate. FmChannel's default scales each burst so its own peak hits the stated deviation; a live link can't see the whole burst first, and a real transmitter doesn't either, so net-sim always drives at fixed gain (FmChannel's `LimitAtDeviationHz` mode). The transmit limiter sits after pre-emphasis and before the audio filter, which is where Tait put it (MMA-00005-05 p.58); FmChannel's code clips after the filter, although its comment describes Tait's order.

## The radios

The defaults are a Tait TM8100/TM8200, from Tait's manuals (document number and page given for each).

| Setting | Default | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Rated deviation | 2.5 / 4 / 5 kHz (narrow / mid / wide) | MMA-00072-03 p.6 |
| IF bandwidth | 7.8 / 12.0 / 12.6 kHz at -3 dB, times 1.25 for the model's -6 dB width | MMA-00005-05 p.73; the 1.25 is FmChannel's assumption |
| Voice path | 300-3000 Hz, 6 dB/octave emphasis, limiter at rated deviation | MMA-00072-03 p.13, p.17 |
| Emphasis time constant | 750 us | Not published by Tait; FmChannel's figure |
| Data path | 20 Hz to half the IF bandwidth, flat | The R1 and T13 taps, MMA-00005-05 p.56 |
| Power | 25 W | MMA-00072-03 p.9, p.18 |
| Noise figure | 6.0 / 5.3 / 5.4 dB | Fitted: puts the model's 12 dB SINAD point at Tait's measured -121 dBm (MMA-00072-03 p.14). Tait publish no noise figure |
| Hum and noise | 42 / 45.5 / 47.5 dB | Middle of Tait's measured range, MMA-00072-03 p.13 |
| Squelch presets | country -115, city -113, hard -107 dBm; hysteresis 9 / 8 / 4 dB | MMA-00072-03 p.14, MMA-00056-09 |
| Site noise | residential | ITU-R P.372, as in FmChannel's link budget |
| Receive level | -18 dBFS at rated deviation | Calibrated so idle hiss is radio1's measured -16 dBFS |

## How it compares with real radios

| | Model | Measured | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Idle open-squelch hiss (narrow data radio) | -16.2 dBFS | -15.9 dBFS | radio1; calibrated, not a check |
| A data signal on that radio | -25.6 dBFS, 9.4 dB under idle | -25 dBFS, about 9 dB under | radio1 |
| A strong unmodulated carrier | -60.5 dBFS | -55 dBFS | radio1 |
| 12 dB SINAD | -121 dBm | -121 dBm | Tait; fitted, not a check |
| 20 dB SINAD (narrow / mid / wide) | -114 / -118 / -119 dBm | -111 / -113 / -113 dBm | Tait, MMA-00072-03 p.14 |
| Co-channel rejection (narrow / wide) | -7.9 / -2.2 dB | -5.7 / -2.7 dB | Tait, MMA-00072-03 p.13 |

The radio1 figures are from pdn-soundmodem's `docs/dev/carrier-sense.md` and its `OpenSquelchFmReceiver` test fixture. The table is regenerated by the tests in `internal/fm` (`go test ./internal/fm -v`).

With real TNCs through the router (Dire Wolf, 1200 baud, default radios, 12 frames per point): every frame at 156 dB of path loss, 9 at 157, 6 at 157.5, 2 at 158 and none at 159. That is the FM threshold: from all to nothing in about 3 dB. Two hidden stations transmitting together into a third, one 20 dB stronger: the stronger was copied 4 times in 5, the weaker never.

## Where it falls short

- **Above threshold it improves too fast.** Fitted to Tait's 12 dB SINAD sensitivity, it reaches 20 dB SINAD 3 to 6 dB sooner than a real Tait. An ideal limiter-discriminator is a better demodulator than a real receiver. Links near the edge are modelled well; how clean a middling link sounds is optimistic.
- **The receive level is calibrated, not physical.** The -18 dBFS default matches radio1's sound card. Another station's card and settings would differ.
- **Squelch is signal strength only.** Tait also offer noise squelch; it isn't modelled. The squelch opens and closes instantly unless `open_ms` and `close_ms` are set, because Tait publish no timing.
- **Not modelled:** transmitter rise and fall time (Tait: under 10 ms), receiver AGC, the receiving sound card's clock offset (FmChannel has it; net-sim's ports share one clock), fading, multipath, Doppler, and adjacent-channel signals.
- **Hum and noise are white.** Real hum is mostly mains harmonics; only the level is Tait's.
- **The rx_decision events are a label.** `capture` and `collision` in the event stream compare received levels against 6 dB for the visualiser. What a TNC hears doesn't depend on that label.

## What this shows about pdn-soundmodem

On an open-squelch receiver, which is how packet stations normally run, the hiss between transmissions is louder than the signal during them. pdn behaves on that as its own documentation says it does on air without a control cable to the radio:

- Its audio-only carrier sense can hold a transmission for about ten seconds after hearing traffic. pdn says so in its start-up log.
- Its c4fsk receiver misses frames; that is pdn issue #518.
- Its qpsk receiver loses some frames. It resets its frequency tracker when the in-band level rises at the start of a burst (`QpskDemodulator.cs`), and on an open-squelch radio the level falls instead, so after a stretch of hiss the tracker has wandered and the preamble can't bring it back. In a run of qpsk3600 frames on a wide channel, the open-squelch pair copied four of five, and the same pair with the squelch closed copied all six and the reply. This one isn't in pdn's documentation.

With the squelch closed (`radio: { squelch: hard }`) all three go away, as they would on air.

One more, with the squelch closed and a strong link: pdn's frequency matching (on by default) costs frames. After a station has heard three frames from another, it trims its transmitter to the offset it measured, and the next reply is sometimes lost. Three frames each way between two pdn stations:

| Mode | Frequency matching off | On |
|---|---|---|
| c4fsk9600 | 6 of 6 | 5 of 6 in one run, 3 of 6 in another |
| qpsk3600 | 6 of 6 | 5 of 6 |

The channel has no frequency error, yet pdn measured offsets from -5 to +7 Hz. The same received audio decodes offline, so it is the live receiver's state or the trimmed transmission, not the channel. net-sim runs pdn from command-line flags, and frequency matching is only in pdn's config file, so it can't be switched off per port yet.

## Changing the model

If M0LTE.FmChannel changes, bump the version in `tools/fmref/fmref.csproj`, run `dotnet run -c Release` in that directory, and run `go test ./internal/fm`. A failure means the port no longer matches the package.
